The Diploma Programme

The IB Diploma Programme is a two-year experience for 11th and 12th graders that prepares them for college and an increasingly global world. Students take a balanced set of demanding courses plus three core components that build critical thinking, research skills and personal growth. There are two pathways: Diploma Candidate and Certificate Student.

To earn the IB Diploma, students:

  • Take six IB courses across subject areas (English, a world language, math, science, history, and an arts or elective option), with 3 to 4 at Higher Level (HL) and the rest at Standard Level (SL).
  • Complete the IB Core: Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay and CAS.
  • Take IB exams at the end, which include written assessments, projects, and sometimes oral presentations or performances.
  • Maintain academic integrity, commitment and steady engagement for both years.

To earn the IB Certificate, students:

  • Take at least two IB courses across subject areas, some at HL and some at SL.
  • Take part in the IB Core: TOK, with CAS as an option.
  • Take the end-of-programme IB exams.
  • Maintain academic integrity, commitment and engagement.

Awarding of the IB Diploma

The IB Diploma is a 2 year course of study. All IB students study 6 subjects from different academic areas of which 3 (or 4) are studied at higher level and 3(or 2) at standard level. IB subjects are graded on a 1- 7 scale with 7 being the highest score. To qualify for the award of the IB Diploma a student must receive a combined score of at least 24 points across 6 subjects with at least 12 points from higher level subjects and a minimum of 9 points from standard level subjects.

In addition to the 6 subjects, IB Diploma students are required to complete three Core elements and the Group 4 project – Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay, CAS (Creativity, Activity, Service), and the Group 4 Project

The maximum points that can be awarded to an IB Diploma student is the full score of 45 with 42 points generated across the 6 subjects and 3 bonus points derived from Theory of Knowledge and the Extended Essay. What is the CORE and how is it assessed?

The Core comprises Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay and CAS (Creativity, Activity, Service).

Theory of Knowledge is a (minimum) 100 hour course taught over 2 years about critical thinking and inquiry into the nature of knowledge. It is assessed through an exhibition of knowledge objects (Internal Assessment Year 1) and a 1600-word essay (External Assessment Year 2).

The Extended Essay is a 4000-word independent research essay into a topic that the student is interested in. The student is guided by a supervisor.

CAS is a journey of self-discovery in which students participate in a range of activities to meet seven learning outcomes.
